THE PROJECT

Connecting marketing leads to what they actually spend

A lead funnel lives in one system. Orders and payments live in another. Most of the time those two never actually get joined — so you can answer "which channel converts the most leads" but not "which channel brings in customers who go on to sell something." This pipeline closes that gap: lead → won deal → seller → first order → revenue, all traceable back to the channel that brought the lead in.

Built on the public Olist Brazilian e-commerce dataset, using Databricks, PySpark, and Spark SQL in a medallion (bronze → silver → gold) architecture.

ARCHITECTURE

Two medallion pipelines that converge at gold

Marketing and commerce data start as separate bronze/silver pipelines and meet at the gold layer, where seller-value tables are attributed back to the lead that acquired them.

LayerMarketing funnelCommerce
Bronze Raw MQL + closed-deals CSVs, ingested as-is with _source_file / _ingested_at lineage columns 9 raw Olist CSVs (sellers, orders, items, payments, products, customers, reviews, geolocation, category translation)
Silver Deduplicated via window functions, categorical fields standardized, timeline anomalies flagged and quarantined — not dropped Orders/items/payments/products joined and rolled up to seller- and order-grain, with revenue reconciled to the cent
Gold Lead-level funnel, channel performance, landing-page performance, monthly trend Acquired-seller performance — post-acquisition orders/revenue only, attributed one record per seller via window-function dedup

Everything lands as managed Delta tables in Unity Catalog (workspace.default.*); raw files sit in a Unity Catalog volume.

Organic search wins on total volume and total revenue, simply because it brings in the most leads. But paid search is the better channel per seller: a smaller lead pool that activates more often (51.8% vs. organic's 41.7%) and earns more per acquired seller once it does (€796 vs. €764). Social pulls in real volume but converts worse than every other channel — the kind of gap that's invisible until lead data and commerce data are actually joined.

SELLER VALUE

Only counting revenue a seller earned after they were acquired

The attribution logic here is the part I'd actually defend in an interview: each seller is matched to their earliest won lead (window-function dedup, ties broken deterministically), and only orders placed on or after that acquisition date count toward their post-acquisition value. Orders that happen to predate the recorded win — a messy-data edge case in the raw dataset — don't inflate the numbers.

DATA QUALITY

Bad rows get flagged and quarantined, not deleted

When the pipeline hits bad data, it doesn't silently drop it. The marketing funnel has exactly one row with a negative sales-cycle-days value — a data-entry issue upstream. It's flagged with has_invalid_sales_timeline, excluded from cycle-time averages, and written to a separate quarantine table rather than deleted outright. Every layer writes its own quality table, and the notebooks assert on the numbers — if silver commerce item revenue doesn't reconcile to seller-level revenue within 0.01, the notebook fails loudly instead of shipping a quietly wrong gold table.

WHAT DIDN'T MAKE THE CUT

Built on Free Edition — that's a real constraint, not a footnote

This runs on Databricks' free single-user workspace: no Jobs/Workflows scheduler, no multi-user access control, no autoscaling cluster. There's no "runs nightly" story here — it's run-the-notebooks-in-order. In a production environment this would be a Delta Live Tables pipeline or a scheduled multi-task Job. The Olist dataset is also a fixed historical snapshot (2017–2018), so the monthly-trend numbers describe that window, not a live feed — the lineage columns and reconciliation asserts are written as if new files would land periodically, but nothing here actually tests that. And there's no BI layer on top: numbers are surfaced through notebook queries, not a dashboard tool — a deliberate scope cut for an interview-facing artifact, not an oversight.

WHAT'S NEXT

If I kept building this

Move it onto a workspace with Jobs and turn the bronze→silver→gold notebooks into a scheduled, properly-ordered pipeline. Add a thin BI layer on the gold tables. Extend seller-value past the first-year window to look at retention and churn, not just activation. Replace the inline asserts with real unit tests for the transformation logic.
